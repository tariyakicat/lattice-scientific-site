## 1. How to Design a Scientific Mechanism Diagram

- Proposed slug: `how-to-design-a-scientific-mechanism-diagram`
- Proposed category: `Mechanism Diagrams`
- Primary audience: researchers, biotech R&D teams, medical affairs teams, materials and environmental science companies
- Search intent: practical how-to / commissioning guidance
- Funnel role: high-intent service education
- Primary keyword: `scientific mechanism diagram`
- Secondary keywords: `mechanism diagram design`, `biological pathway diagram`, `scientific pathway illustration`, `mechanism of action illustration`
- Proposed title: `How to Design a Scientific Mechanism Diagram`
- Meta description: `Plan a clear scientific mechanism diagram with the right scope, visual hierarchy, review workflow, labels, and publication-ready outputs.`
- Quick answer draft: `A strong mechanism diagram shows the minimum set of actors, relationships, and outcomes needed to explain one scientific claim. Begin with a written causal sequence, separate evidence from interpretation, choose a structure that matches the mechanism, and review scientific accuracy before detailed rendering or publication export.`

### Proposed article structure

1. `What is a scientific mechanism diagram?`
   - Direct answer: Define it as a causal explanation, not a decorative collection of scientific objects.
2. `What should a mechanism diagram include?`
   - Direct answer: Include only actors, transformations, conditions, and outcomes required by the central claim.
3. `How do you turn a written mechanism into a visual sequence?`
   - Direct answer: Convert each causal statement into an actor-action-result unit before choosing the composition.
4. `Which layout works for pathways, cycles, and multiscale systems?`
   - Add a comparison table for linear, cyclic, branching, spatial, and multiscale structures.
5. `How should arrows, colours, and labels be used?`
   - Cover arrow semantics, line styles, legends, accessible colour, abbreviations, and visual grammar.
6. `How do you review a mechanism diagram for scientific accuracy?`
   - Provide a two-stage content review and visual review checklist.
7. `When should you commission a bespoke mechanism illustration?`
   - Compare DIY diagrams, icon libraries, and custom 2D/3D production.

### FAQ candidates

- What is the difference between a mechanism diagram and a graphical abstract?
- How many steps should a mechanism diagram contain?
- Can a mechanism diagram include unproven hypotheses?
- What file formats should be delivered for publication?

## 2. Data Visualization for Scientific Research

- Proposed slug: `data-visualization-for-scientific-research`
- Proposed category: `Data Visualization`
- Primary audience: research groups, biotech and materials companies, programme leads, analysts, grant and impact teams
- Search intent: informational / design decision support
- Funnel role: authority building for data storytelling projects
- Primary keyword: `data visualization for scientific research`
- Secondary keywords: `scientific data visualization`, `research data storytelling`, `figure design`, `data visualization for reports`
- Proposed title: `Data Visualization for Scientific Research: A Practical Guide`
- Meta description: `Choose clearer charts, visual hierarchy, annotations, and review methods for scientific data visualizations in papers, presentations, and reports.`
- Quick answer draft: `Scientific data visualization should preserve the evidence while helping a specific audience compare values, see uncertainty, and identify the relevant pattern. Start with the decision or question, choose an honest visual encoding, retain necessary context, label directly where possible, and test whether the chart remains understandable without a spoken explanation.`

### Proposed article structure

1. `What makes scientific data visualization different?`
   - Direct answer: It must support interpretation without hiding uncertainty or changing the evidence.
2. `What question should the chart answer?`
   - Direct answer: Write the intended comparison or decision before selecting a chart type.
3. `Which chart should you use for comparison, distribution, change, and relationship?`
   - Add a semantic comparison table of chart tasks and common failure modes.
4. `How should uncertainty and sample size be shown?`
   - Cover intervals, distributions, raw points, denominators, and uncertainty language.
5. `How do you build visual hierarchy without distorting the data?`
   - Cover emphasis, colour, annotation, scales, axis baselines, and small multiples.
6. `How should one dataset change across papers, slides, and impact reports?`
   - Explain format-specific editing without changing the underlying claim.
7. `What should a scientific data visualization review check?`
   - Add accuracy, accessibility, reproducibility, and export checklist.

- Primary keyword: `scientific presentation slides`
- Secondary keywords: `research presentation design`, `scientific slide deck`, `biotech presentation design`, `conference slides`
- Proposed title: `How to Design Scientific Presentation Slides People Can Follow`
- Meta description: `Structure scientific presentation slides for clarity, with better pacing, data stories, visual hierarchy, and audience-specific narrative decisions.`
- Quick answer draft: `Effective scientific presentation slides support a spoken argument instead of reproducing a paper on screen. Give each slide one job, organise the deck around audience questions, reveal evidence in a controlled sequence, simplify charts for the room, and design the final slides for the actual screen, timing, and discussion context.`

### Proposed article structure

1. `What makes a scientific presentation easy to follow?`
   - Direct answer: A visible argument and controlled information pace matter more than the number of slides.
2. `How should a scientific slide deck be structured?`
   - Direct answer: Organise around context, decision, evidence, implication, and next step rather than manuscript headings.
3. `How much information should one slide contain?`
   - Direct answer: One slide should advance one communicative job, even when it contains several related visual elements.
4. `How do you redesign a paper figure for a presentation?`
   - Add a paper-versus-slide comparison table.
5. `How should decks differ for conferences, investors, and internal R&D?`
   - Compare audience knowledge, evidence depth, pacing, and CTA.
6. `How do you make scientific slides accessible?`
   - Cover contrast, type size, colour, narration, alt text in distributed decks, and readable exports.
7. `What should the final rehearsal test?`
   - Add timing, screen, click path, backup, and Q&A checklist.

- Quick answer draft: `An interactive research microsite is useful when a project needs more context, exploration, or reuse than a static paper, poster, or PDF can provide. It should be designed around audience tasks, use progressive disclosure, preserve source context, load quickly, work without fragile interactions, and have a clear owner for maintenance after launch.`

### Proposed article structure

1. `What is an interactive research microsite?`
   - Direct answer: A focused digital experience that explains one project, programme, dataset, or impact story.
2. `When is a microsite better than a PDF or project page?`
   - Add a microsite-versus-PDF-versus-page comparison table.
3. `Who is the microsite for and what should they be able to do?`
   - Define researcher, partner, investor, policymaker, and public tasks.
4. `What content should an interactive research microsite include?`
   - Cover summary, findings, methods, data, people, outputs, downloads, references, and contact.
5. `How should interactivity support the research story?`
   - Explain progressive disclosure, filtering, guided exploration, animation, and graceful fallback.
6. `What technical and accessibility requirements matter?`
   - Cover performance, mobile layout, keyboard support, reduced motion, alt text, metadata, and analytics.
7. `Who maintains the microsite after launch?`
   - Cover ownership, updates, expiry, archived versions, domain, and governance.

## 5. How to Design a Research Impact Report

- Proposed slug: `research-impact-report-design-guide`
- Proposed category: `Research Impact`
- Primary audience: funded programmes, research institutes, corporate R&D, sustainability teams, universities, charities
- Search intent: planning / examples / commissioning
- Funnel role: higher-value B2B lead generation
- Primary keyword: `research impact report design`
- Secondary keywords: `research impact report`, `grant impact report`, `R&D impact report`, `research report design`
- Proposed title: `Research Impact Report Design: From Evidence to a Clear Story`
- Meta description: `Plan a credible research impact report with a clear audience, evidence hierarchy, case studies, data visualizations, and digital reuse.`
- Quick answer draft: `A strong research impact report connects activity, evidence, outcomes, and future value without turning the document into a list of outputs. Define the decisions the report must support, separate verified results from interpretation, build a visible evidence hierarchy, use case studies selectively, and design each section for both continuous reading and rapid scanning.`

### Proposed article structure

1. `What is a research impact report?`
   - Direct answer: It explains what changed, for whom, through what evidence, and why the change matters.
2. `Who is the impact report for?`
   - Direct answer: The audience determines the evidence depth, language, format, and call to action.
3. `How should an impact report be structured?`
   - Cover executive answer, context, activity, outcomes, evidence, cases, limitations, and next steps.
4. `How do you distinguish outputs, outcomes, and impact?`
   - Add a semantic comparison table with examples and evidence requirements.
5. `Which data visualizations belong in an impact report?`
   - Cover denominators, timelines, maps, distributions, networks, and transparent sourcing.
6. `How should case studies support the evidence?`
   - Explain selection, consent, attribution, representative limits, and narrative role.
7. `Should the report be a PDF, microsite, or both?`
   - Compare accessibility, discoverability, permanence, interaction, print, and maintenance.
8. `What should an impact-report QA review include?`
   - Add claims, references, permissions, accessibility, export, web, and update-date checklist.
